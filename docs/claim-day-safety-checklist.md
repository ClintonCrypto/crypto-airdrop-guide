# Claim-Day Safety Checklist for Airdrop Hunters

> A claim is the moment scammers want you rushed. Slow down, verify the link, and never sign what you cannot read. This checklist covers the hours before, during, and after any token claim.

## 1. Before claim day — verify the source

- [ ] Get the claim link ONLY from the project's official X account, official docs, or official Discord/Telegram announcement. Never from a DM, a reply, a search ad, or a "support" account.
- [ ] Check the domain character by character. Attackers register lookalikes (across.to was hijacked in Nov 2026 and swapped for a phishing site).
- [ ] Confirm the claim window on the official announcement: start date, end date, and timezone. Deadlines move — re-check the day you claim.
- [ ] Know what you are claiming and whether you are eligible. Some claims are "claim check only" — e.g. Aster Stage 6 is only for people who farmed Stage 6 and chose full vesting.
- [ ] Bookmark the official claim page once you have verified it. Use the bookmark, not a link from a post, on claim day.

## 2. Deadlines on the board right now (verify on official channels before you act)

- Extended (XTEN): allocation checker + terms-signing window open 11 Nov–31 Dec 2026, 23:59 UTC. Sign the Community Distribution Terms in the Extended app to see your allocation (~4.29 XTEN per Xpoint). TGE date still TBA — the claim itself is NOT open yet.

## 3. Wallet setup — isolate the claim

- [ ] Claim from a separate wallet, not the wallet holding your main funds. Move the claim there first, or claim into a fresh wallet.
- [ ] Never paste a seed phrase or private key anywhere to claim. No real claim asks for it.
- [ ] Check the wallet's connected sites and revoke old approvals before claim day (a stale approval is how drained wallets happen).
- [ ] If you log in with Google/Apple (social login), remember: that is not self-custody. The Nov 2026 Keplr/Web3Auth incident drained social-login wallets while seed-phrase and hardware wallets were untouched.

## 4. On the claim site — read before you sign

- [ ] Read every wallet prompt in full. A claim should ask for a simple claim transaction — not an unlimited token approval, not a "setApprovalForAll", not a blind message signature.
- [ ] If the site asks for an approval you do not understand, STOP. Close the tab and ask in the project's official channel.
- [ ] Check the gas fee. A claim that wants an absurd fee, or routes through an unknown contract, is a red flag.
- [ ] Verify the contract address on a block explorer before you approve anything, when the project publishes one.

## 5. After you claim — clean up

- [ ] Revoke the claim site's approval once the tokens are in your wallet.
- [ ] Move the tokens to cold storage or your main wallet; do not leave them sitting in the claim wallet.
- [ ] Ignore every "claim again", "double your airdrop", or "support" message that arrives after. Real projects do not DM you about claims.
- [ ] If the claim site looked off, or you approved something strange: revoke immediately, move remaining funds to a fresh wallet, and warn others in the project's official channel.

## 6. If something goes wrong

1. Revoke the approval on a revocation tool or in your wallet's approvals view.
2. Move any remaining funds to a fresh wallet you control.
3. Do not send funds to any "recovery" service — those are the same scammers twice.
4. Report the phishing domain to the project team and to the wallet's phishing list.

## Red flags — walk away if you see any

- The claim link arrived by DM, email, or a search ad.
- The site asks for your seed phrase, private key, or a "wallet sync".
- The domain is close but not exact, or the site appeared hours ago.
- The claim promises a bonus for claiming fast, or for connecting a second wallet.
- The project's official X has said nothing about a claim.
