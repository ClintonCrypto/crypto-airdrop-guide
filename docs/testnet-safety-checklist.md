# Testnet Safety Checklist for Airdrop Hunters

Testnets feel risk-free because the tokens are "fake" — but most testnet scams are not after testnet tokens. They are after your real wallet, your real approvals, and your mainnet funds. Run through this checklist before you connect a wallet to any testnet task.

## 1. Use a throwaway wallet

- Create a **separate wallet** only for testnets and airdrop farming. Never farm with the wallet that holds your main funds.
- Keep only small amounts of real gas money in the farming wallet — treat anything in it as spendable, not savings.
- Use a different browser profile (or a separate browser) for farming, so a malicious site never sees your main wallet's extensions.

## 2. Verify the source before you click

- Start from the project's **official X account, site, or docs** — never from a random reply, DM, or "guide" account.
- Check the handle letter by letter. Scam accounts copy names with one character changed.
- Bookmark the real site. Faucet and claim pages are the most copied pages in crypto.
- If a "claim" or "testnet" link arrives by DM, it is a scam. Projects do not DM claim links.

## 3. Watch what you sign

- Read every signature request. A plain message signature is usually harmless; a transaction is not.
- **Unlimited token approvals are the biggest silent risk.** If a testnet dApp asks for unlimited approval of a real token, stop.
- After farming a project, revoke approvals you no longer need (for example with revoke.cash) — on mainnet networks especially.
- Never sign anything that asks you to "verify" by sending funds first.

## 4. Protect your keys

- No legit testnet, faucet, or airdrop will **ever** ask for your seed phrase or private key. Anyone who asks is stealing.
- Do not paste your seed phrase into any website, form, or "support" chat — including screenshots of it.
- Keep the farming wallet's seed separate from your main wallet's seed. One leak should never cost you everything.

## 5. Custody reminder

- Testnet points and tokens have **no guaranteed value**. Farm with time, not with money you cannot lose.
- When a real airdrop or claim opens, move rewards to your main cold wallet promptly — exchanges and farming wallets are not vaults.
- Screenshot and save your important wallet addresses and transaction hashes; claims support often asks for them.

## 6. If an exchange you use gets hacked

- Don't take a "contained" or "under control" statement at face value. In November 2026, Mercado Bitcoin said its breach was contained — on-chain data later showed the attacker kept draining customer deposit addresses for about four hours afterwards.
- Watch the money, not the statement: check official status updates and independent on-chain trackers before you assume your funds are safe.
- Exchanges usually reopen in phases (view-only first, then withdrawals). Wait for the official reopening notice — don't rush funds through unofficial links.
- When withdrawals reopen, move long-term holdings to self-custody promptly. An exchange account is not a vault.
- Beware fake "reimbursement" or "claim" pages that appear after a big hack. Only use links from the exchange's official account or status page.

## Red flags — walk away immediately

- 🚩 "Send 0.1 ETH to unlock your testnet reward"
- 🚩 A claim page that is not linked from the project's official account
- 🚩 Countdown pressure: "claim in the next 10 minutes or lose it"
- 🚩 Support agents asking for your seed phrase or remote access to your device
- 🚩 A faucet that wants you to download an unknown app or browser extension
